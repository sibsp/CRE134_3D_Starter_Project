=PAGE Colliders and Physics
=SUB Make the world solid: what blocks the player, invisible walls and ramps, and crates that obey gravity and mass.

Up to Step 04 you could walk straight through trees, rocks and animals. They were only pictures. Step 05 gives them <b>colliders</b>, invisible shapes the physics engine uses to decide what touches what. It also adds an invisible boundary, a hidden ramp on the tower stairs and three crates with <b>Rigidbodies</b> that you can push around.

=IMG step05_crates|The three crates by the gate: 10 kg, 40 kg and 200 kg. Same size, same material, very different weight.

• <b>You will learn:</b> the difference between what you see (renderer) and what you hit (collider); collider shapes and their costs; static vs dynamic objects; Rigidbody mass and gravity; how the player controller collides; invisible colliders as design tools; collision code.

=PAGE The big idea
A game object has two separate “bodies”. The <b>visual</b> one (the Mesh Renderer) is what you see: it can have thousands of triangles. The <b>physical</b> one (the Collider) is what the physics engine uses: usually a much simpler shape like a box, sphere or capsule. Checking whether two simple shapes overlap is fast; checking two detailed meshes is slow.

The two don't have to match. A tree's leafy canopy has no collider at all (you can't climb it anyway), but its trunk has a simple invisible <b>capsule</b>. That's cheaper, and it feels better: the player slides smoothly around the trunk instead of snagging on bark.

<b>Physics</b> is the next layer. Adding a <b>Rigidbody</b> to an object hands it over to the physics engine: it now has mass, falls under gravity, can be pushed and tumbles when it hits things.

=BOX #F2B65C|Real-world link
A theatre set is mostly painted flats. The audience sees a castle wall; the actors know which bits are solid enough to lean on. Colliders are the “solid bits” of your set, and they don't have to look like anything.
=END

=PAGE Key words
<b><color=#F2B65C>Collider</color></b>: An invisible shape that defines where an object is solid.

<b><color=#F2B65C>Box / Sphere / Capsule Collider</color></b>: Simple, fast primitive shapes. Use these whenever you can.

<b><color=#F2B65C>Mesh Collider</color></b>: Uses an actual mesh as its shape. Accurate but more expensive; best for static scenery like the ground or walls.

<b><color=#F2B65C>Convex</color></b>: A Mesh Collider option that wraps the mesh in a simplified shell with no dents. Required if the object has a Rigidbody.

<b><color=#F2B65C>Static collider</color></b>: A collider with no Rigidbody. Never moves: ground, walls, trees.

<b><color=#F2B65C>Rigidbody</color></b>: Makes an object dynamic: it has mass and responds to gravity, forces and collisions.

<b><color=#F2B65C>Mass</color></b>: How heavy it is (in kilograms). Heavier things are harder to get moving.

<b><color=#F2B65C>Is Kinematic</color></b>: A Rigidbody you move by script that pushes others but isn't pushed back.

<b><color=#F2B65C>Is Trigger</color></b>: A collider you can pass through that reports when something enters it (used heavily in Step 09).

<b><color=#F2B65C>Character Controller</color></b>: A special capsule collider for players: it walks, climbs steps and slides along walls, but isn't moved by physics.

=PAGE How Unity does it
Select an object and click <b>Add Component</b> › Physics › Box Collider (or Sphere, Capsule, Mesh). A green wireframe appears in the Scene view showing the collider shape. Click <b>Edit Collider</b> to drag its faces, or type Center and Size.

Add <b>Add Component</b> › Physics › <b>Rigidbody</b> to make something fall and get pushed. The settings that matter most:

<b>Mass</b>: Weight in kg. Default 1. Doesn't change how fast things fall (just like real life), but changes how hard they are to push.

<b>Linear Damping</b>: Air resistance / friction slowing movement. 0 = slides forever on ice.

<b>Angular Damping</b>: How quickly spinning slows down.

<b>Use Gravity</b>: Tick to fall.

<b>Is Kinematic</b>: Tick to move it only by script or animation (a moving platform).

<b>Constraints</b>: Freeze movement or rotation on chosen axes, e.g. stop a crate tipping over.

=BOX #7FC6E0|Good to know
The player uses a <b>Character Controller</b>, not a Rigidbody. It stops at walls and walks up small steps (Step Offset 0.25 m) and slopes up to its Slope Limit (45°), but physics forces don't move it. To push crates, the Starter Assets add a small script, <b>Basic Rigid Body Push</b>, which you'll meet in the code section.
=END

=PAGE Build it yourself
<color=#F2B65C><b>1.</b></color> <b>Turn on pushing.</b> Select <b>PlayerCapsule</b> (inside <i>Player (First Person)</i>). On the <b>Basic Rigid Body Push</b> component, tick <b>Can Push</b> and set <b>Push Layers</b> to <b>Default</b> (the crates' layer). Without this, the player walks into crates but can't move them.

<color=#F2B65C><b>2.</b></color> Press Play and push each crate. Feel the difference. Stop Play.

<color=#F2B65C><b>3.</b></color> Make your own crate: GameObject › 3D Object › <b>Cube</b>. It already has a Box Collider. Give it the LG_Wood material.

<color=#F2B65C><b>4.</b></color> Add Component › <b>Rigidbody</b>. Set Mass to 5. Raise the cube 3 m into the air and press Play. It falls and lands.

<color=#F2B65C><b>5.</b></color> Put a tree without a collider in your scene (or disable a <b>Trunk Collider</b>). Walk through it. Then add a <b>Capsule Collider</b>, click <b>Edit Collider</b> and shape it to the trunk only.

<color=#F2B65C><b>6.</b></color> Make a ramp: a Cube rotated 20° on X. Walk up it. Try 50°: the Character Controller's Slope Limit stops you.

<color=#F2B65C><b>7.</b></color> Make an invisible wall: a Cube where you want to block the player, then <b>untick its Mesh Renderer</b>. The collider stays.

=BOX #E58A7A|Watch out
Never scale a Rigidbody object to a negative size, and avoid Mesh Colliders on moving objects unless Convex is ticked. Both cause physics glitches. And don't use invisible walls in places a player would reasonably expect to walk: it feels unfair. Use fences, rocks or water to <i>show</i> the boundary where you can.
=END

=PAGE The code
This is the Starter Assets script that lets the player push crates. It runs whenever the Character Controller bumps into something.

=CODE BasicRigidBodyPush.cs (Starter Assets, simplified comments removed)
public class BasicRigidBodyPush : MonoBehaviour
{
    public LayerMask pushLayers;
    public bool canPush;
    [Range(0.5f, 5f)] public float strength = 1.1f;

    private void OnControllerColliderHit(ControllerColliderHit hit)
    {
        if (canPush) PushRigidBodies(hit);
    }

    private void PushRigidBodies(ControllerColliderHit hit)
    {
        Rigidbody body = hit.collider.attachedRigidbody;
        if (body == null || body.isKinematic) return;

        var bodyLayerMask = 1 << body.gameObject.layer;
        if ((bodyLayerMask & pushLayers.value) == 0) return;

        if (hit.moveDirection.y < -0.3f) return;

        Vector3 pushDir = new Vector3(hit.moveDirection.x, 0.0f, hit.moveDirection.z);
        body.AddForce(pushDir * strength, ForceMode.Impulse);
    }
}
=END

<b>3–5</b>: Settings: which layers can be pushed, an on/off switch, and how hard to push. <b>[Range]</b> turns the number into a slider.

<b>7</b>: <b>OnControllerColliderHit</b> is called by Unity every time the Character Controller touches a collider while moving.

<b>14–15</b>: Get the Rigidbody of what we hit. If there isn't one (a wall) or it's kinematic, stop here.

<b>17–18</b>: A <b>LayerMask</b> check: only push objects on the chosen layers. This is why Push Layers must include Default.

<b>20</b>: Don't push things we're standing on (moving downwards into them).

<b>22</b>: Push only horizontally, in the direction we're walking.

<b>23</b>: <b>AddForce</b> with <b>ForceMode.Impulse</b> gives a sudden shove. The physics engine divides it by mass, which is why the 200 kg crate barely moves.

=BOX #7FC6E0|Good to know
This script was written by Unity, not for this course, so it uses a more compact “professional” style than our own scripts: one-line <b>if</b> statements, <b>var</b>, and <b>&lt;&lt;</b> (a bit-shift used for layer masks). You don't need to write code like this. Our own scripts spell every step out on its own line.
=END

And here's a script you can add to a crate yourself. It reacts when something hits it hard:

=CODE CrateThud.cs
using UnityEngine;

// CRATE THUD: prints a message when something hits this crate hard.
public class CrateThud : MonoBehaviour
{
    // Unity calls this when another collider bumps into this one.
    // "collision" holds the details of the hit.
    private void OnCollisionEnter(Collision collision)
    {
        // How fast the two objects hit each other, in metres per second.
        float speed = collision.relativeVelocity.magnitude;

        // Ignore gentle bumps.
        if (speed > 2f)
        {
            string otherName = collision.gameObject.name;
            Debug.Log(name + " hit " + otherName + " at " + speed.ToString("0.0") + " m/s");
        }
    }
}
=END

<b>OnCollisionEnter</b> runs when two colliders hit and at least one has a non-kinematic Rigidbody. <b>relativeVelocity.magnitude</b> is how fast they hit each other. In Step 06 you could play a thud sound here instead of logging.

=PAGE Common problems
<b>I can't push the crates.</b><br><indent=4%>On PlayerCapsule › Basic Rigid Body Push: tick Can Push and include the crates' layer in Push Layers.</indent>

<b>Object falls through the floor.</b><br><indent=4%>The floor has no collider, or the object has a Rigidbody but no collider.</indent>

<b>Rigidbody with Mesh Collider throws an error.</b><br><indent=4%>Tick <b>Convex</b> on the Mesh Collider, or use a Box/Capsule instead.</indent>

<b>Player gets stuck on stairs.</b><br><indent=4%>Add an invisible ramp collider, or increase Step Offset on the Character Controller.</indent>

<b>Crate jitters or explodes away on Play.</b><br><indent=4%>It starts overlapping another collider. Lift it slightly so it begins in free space.</indent>

<b>OnCollisionEnter never runs.</b><br><indent=4%>Neither object has a non-kinematic Rigidbody, or one is set to Is Trigger (use OnTriggerEnter instead).</indent>

=PAGE Answers
<color=#9fe3ff><b>A1</b></color>  It's much cheaper to calculate and feels smoother; the canopy doesn't need to be solid.

<color=#9fe3ff><b>A2</b></color>  Adding a Rigidbody (it also needs a collider).

<color=#9fe3ff><b>A3</b></color>  No. Gravity accelerates them equally; mass changes how hard they are to push, not how fast they fall.

<color=#9fe3ff><b>A4</b></color>  Four box colliders with no renderers that stop the player leaving the play area.

<color=#9fe3ff><b>A5</b></color>  So the player walks smoothly up a slope instead of catching on each step.
